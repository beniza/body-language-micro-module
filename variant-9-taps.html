Lesson 1 · Physical Posture

How you carry your body

  • Face the speaker and give them your full attention.
  • Stand or sit with an open, upright posture to project confidence.
  • Avoid closed-off behaviors such as crossed arms, slouching, or looking down.
  • Invite participation through an open posture and welcoming orientation toward others.

Quick check

A colleague is sharing a concern. Which posture says “I’m listening”?

Face the speaker with an open, upright posture. Crossed arms and looking down can read as closed off.

Lesson 2 · Physical Distance

Where you position yourself

  • Respect personal space while remaining approachable.
  • Lean slightly forward to signal interest and engagement while listening.
  • Adapt your position and proximity to help others feel comfortable and included.

Lesson 3 · Facial Expression

What your face communicates

  • Maintain appropriate eye contact to communicate honesty and attentiveness.
  • Nod occasionally and use appropriate facial expressions to show understanding.
  • Smile genuinely when appropriate to create a welcoming atmosphere.
  • Match your facial expressions to the emotional tone of the conversation.

Quick check

A colleague tells you some difficult news. What should your face do?

Match the tone. Smile genuinely when it fits; in a hard conversation, a calm, attentive face shows you understand.

Lesson 4 · Demeanor

The tone you project

  • Ensure your body language matches the tone of the words you are saying.
  • Show respect by avoiding behaviors that suggest impatience or distraction.
  • Maintain a calm, steady presence, especially during difficult conversations.
  • Use natural, purposeful hand gestures to reinforce important ideas.

True or false?

Glancing at your phone while someone talks is fine, as long as you keep nodding.

False. Show respect by avoiding behaviors that suggest impatience or distraction. Nodding doesn’t cancel out a wandering eye.

Lesson 5 · Cultural Differences

Tendencies, not rules

Be aware of cultural differences in gestures, eye contact, personal space, and levels of formality.

The following points describe general cultural tendencies, not universal rules. Individual preferences, regional customs, and organizational culture should always be considered.

When in doubt, observe first, adapt respectfully, and ask questions when appropriate.

Lesson 6 · Formal vs. Informal

Posture across cultures

  • Western workplaces often encourage relaxed but attentive posture and expressive gestures.
  • Many Eastern cultures place greater emphasis on formal posture, controlled movements, and showing respect through physical demeanor.
When in doubt, begin with a more formal posture and adjust as you observe the communication style of those around you.

Scenario

You meet a senior leader from a culture you don’t know well. How do you start?

Start formal, then adjust. It’s easier to relax a formal start than to recover from one that felt too casual.

Lesson 7 · Personal Distance

How close is comfortable?

  • Many Western cultures (North America, Northern Europe) generally prefer more personal space during conversation.
  • Many Eastern cultures are often more comfortable standing closer to people they know well, though preferences vary widely by country and context.
Observe and adapt to the other person’s comfort level rather than assuming your own preference is universal.

Lesson 8 · Eye Contact

Where to look

  • Many Western cultures view direct eye contact as a sign of confidence, honesty, and engagement.
  • In some Eastern cultures, prolonged eye contact—particularly with elders or authority figures—may be viewed as disrespectful or confrontational.
Aim for comfortable, respectful eye contact while remaining sensitive to cultural expectations.

Quick check

In some Eastern cultures, long eye contact with an elder may be seen as…

Disrespectful or confrontational. What signals honesty in one place can signal challenge in another, so aim for comfortable, respectful eye contact.

Recap

Four things to remember

  • Face the speaker with an open, upright posture.
  • Respect personal space; lean in to listen.
  • Let your face and body match your words.
  • Across cultures: start formal, observe, adapt, ask.
